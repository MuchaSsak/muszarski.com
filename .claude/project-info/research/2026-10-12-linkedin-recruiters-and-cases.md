# LinkedIn for getting hired: how recruiters read profiles, real cases, outreach, posts, Poland

**Summary:** recruiters skim the LinkedIn top card for a few seconds, and Polish juniors still land most jobs through job boards and referrals. So LinkedIn's job for Mateusz is to make him findable and believable at first glance, and to grow ties with about 10 mutual connections that lead to referrals and CTO conversations.
**Accessed 2026-10-12** (every source below). Out of scope (other researchers): LinkedIn search ranking, field limits, the feed algorithm, and Polish job-ad keywords. Labels: **[study]** controlled or peer-reviewed · **[survey]** with n · **[LinkedIn]** published by LinkedIn from internal data, method not disclosed · **[vendor]** a company's own dataset · **[case]** first-person write-up with numbers · **[anecdote]** · **[opinion]** · **[assumption]** my inference.

## Rules we can act on

1. **The top card carries the decision: photo, headline (role + stack), location + remote/hybrid, current role, education.** In TheLadders' eye-tracking study, recruiters made the fit/no-fit call in about 6 s. About 80% of their gaze went to name, titles, companies, dates and education. On LinkedIn profiles, 19% went to the photo [study, vendor, n=30, 2012] (S1).
2. **Use a real, recent photo and get the free verification badge.** LinkedIn says a photo brings up to 21× more profile views and 9× more connection requests. Verified profiles get about 60% more profile views and 30% more connection requests [LinkedIn] (S2, S3). Gartner predicts that 1 in 4 candidate profiles will be fake by 2028, so identity signals matter more now [opinion/forecast] (S4).
3. **Turn on Open to Work (recruiters only at least), with exact titles, cities, remote/hybrid and a start date.** LinkedIn's figures: 2× more recruiter messages; the public frame brings 40% more InMails; Open to Work candidates answer InMails 37% more often [LinkedIn] (S5, S6, S7). The "red flag" view comes from ex-FAANG recruiters. In Hung Lee's poll, 70% called the frame good and 7% bad [opinion/poll] (S8). He has no employer to hide from, so the upside wins [assumption].
4. **Every "open to work" post states stack, city, remote/hybrid and B2B/UoP.** A Polish mid-level front-end dev posted once and got 87 contacts in 1 day, including 22 relevant offers. He had to turn down 12 because the post didn't say React-only, Warsaw-only or B2B-only [case, 2022 market] (S9).
5. **Grow from 8 to about 200 connections in 4 weeks, favouring 2nd-degree people with mutual connections.** Randomized experiments on 20M+ LinkedIn users showed that moderately weak ties (about 10 mutual connections) helped job mobility most, and weak ties help most in digital industries [study, Science 2022] (S10).
6. **Respect the invite limit.** LinkedIn publishes no number; restrictions come from ignored, pending or spam-marked invites and usually last a week; a withdrawn invite can't be re-sent for 3 weeks; free accounts get 5 invite notes a month [LinkedIn] (S11, S12). Outreach tools observe about 100 a week, rolling [opinion] (S13). **Plan: ≤15 invites a day, ≤70–80 a week, and spend the 5 notes on CTOs/EMs.** A note barely changes acceptance (26.42% vs 26.37% over 20M+ attempts) but roughly doubles replies (9.36% vs 5.44%) [vendor] (S14).
7. **Write to hiring managers and CTOs one to one, in under 400 characters.** LinkedIn's InMail data: messages under 400 characters get 22% more replies than average, those over 1,200 get 11% fewer, and 1:1 messages beat bulk ones by about 15% [LinkedIn, tens of millions of InMails, 2021–22] (S7). In interviewing.io's survey, cold outreach to hiring managers was "effective" and to recruiters "ineffective", and it tells juniors to go to hiring managers [survey, n≈500, mostly seniors, directional] (S15). Shape: specific common ground → 1–2 proofs (app live on both stores, a case-study link) → ask for a 15-minute chat, not a job.
8. **Follow target software houses' company pages and engage with their posts.** LinkedIn Recruiter has an "Engaged with your talent brand" Spotlight filter, and recruiters who use Spotlights saw 64% higher InMail replies [LinkedIn, 2017] (S16).
9. **LinkedIn is the trust layer and the referral engine, not the place to apply.** Polish juniors found their last job through job portals 56% of the time [survey, n=811, agency-run, Oct–Dec 2024] (S17). In Traffit's 2024 ATS data, LinkedIn brought 22% of applications but 4% of hires; the company's own candidate database brought 28% [vendor, 4M applications] (S18). 2025 hiring managers call LinkedIn inbound "noisy", source most hires and rate referrals best [interviews, n=30+] (S19). A referral reaches interview 40% of the time vs 3% for inbound (`2026-10-05-hiring-perspective.md`, S6).
10. **Posts show real work (screenshots, short clips, a PDF case study), written by him.** No study ties post format to hiring. Documents lead engagement in Socialinsider's company-page data but have the lowest reach in Ordinal's [vendor] (S20, S21). Likely-AI posts got 45% less engagement [vendor] (S22). One outbound link cuts median reach by 18.8% (S23), but Ordinal sees "almost none" of that penalty on personal profiles and says link-in-comments costs clicks [vendor] (S24). → Link muszarski.com in the body when the click matters; A/B test link-in-comments once.
11. **Expect weeks, and log weekly:** profile views, search appearances, acceptance rate, replies, interviews. A profile rewrite moved weekly search appearances from about 190 to 355 [anecdote] (S25). A student's 30-day push brought 47 recruiter messages, +340% profile views and 3 interviews, with no offer reported [anecdote] (S26).

## 1. How recruiters and hiring managers look at a profile

- **Speed:** no LinkedIn-published dwell-time data exists. Beyond TheLadders (S1): an ATS vendor's eye tracking of 1 recruiter gave about 3 s for a sparse profile, "a few dozen" for a full one [anecdote] (S41); an ex-Amazon recruiter spends about 25× longer on LinkedIn than on a CV [anecdote] (S38).
- **What makes them message:** Open to Work (S5–S7), "likely to respond" or brand-engagement signals (S16). Junior profiles that get reach-outs show pedigree, practical projects and AI-native skills [interviews, Wellfound via S19].
- **Red flags for juniors (mostly opinion):** dates or titles that don't match the CV (recruiters verify on LinkedIn; S30); no photo, activity or connections, which reads as fake (S3, S4); "Aspiring…" or "szukam pracy" in the headline, seen as desperate [opinion] (S40); generic AI posts (S22). CareerBuilder 2018: 70% of US employers screen social media, and 57% of those found content that cost the candidate the job [survey, dated] (S37).
- **Context:** an RCT found that LinkedIn training raised employment by 7 pp (+10%) for at least 12 months, through researching employers and referrals [study, South Africa, 2022] (S27). HackerRank 2018: the portfolio ranked second; 80% of small companies valued it vs 66% of large ones [survey, dated] (S28).
- **Polish developers' side:** 25.5% never open InMails, and 34% open them only for an interesting subject [survey 2023, n not stated] (S29). daily.dev: 43% ignore recruiter outreach, 40% because it "looks like spam"; devs prefer introductions through peers [survey, n=4,040, Dec 2025] (S30).

## 3. Outreach and network

- **Numbers:** rules 5–8. Premium Career gives unlimited invite notes plus 5 InMails a month for about $30–40 (sources disagree); one month may pay off during the push [opinion] (S12, S39).
- **8 connections:** almost no one sees his posts or reaches him through a shared connection until the network grows; mechanics belong to the feed researcher [assumption].
- **Plan:** week 1, everyone he has worked or studied with (Neoteric, Cetuspro, school, Erasmus+/ESC, hackathons). Weeks 2–4, 60–80 invites a week to their colleagues, Tricity developers, EMs/CTOs of software houses and Tricity tech recruiters; accept everyone who engages. Withdraw invites pending for 2–3 weeks. Sales outreach averages 28.5% acceptance (Expandi, 13.2M requests) [vendor] (S14); peers should accept more [assumption].

## 4. Posts

Measured data covers only engagement and reach (rule 10); nothing measures hooks, build-in-public posts or "this post got me hired" for developers. interviewing.io counts a well-received post (a few hundred likes) as social proof for a cold message to a hiring manager [opinion] (S15), so a case-study post works twice: in the feed, then as the link in DMs.

## 5. Poland and the Tricity

- **Juniors are scarce:** 4.79% of IT ads vs over 51% for seniors; juniors often need over 100 applications for a few interviews [JJIT 2026 report, via blog] (S31). Software houses: 73% of applications were for senior roles, and recruiters sourced 22% of candidates by hand [vendor, Traffit, Jan–Oct 2024] (S32).
- **Channels:** among the top three platforms, LinkedIn brought 33% of IT applications, Pracuj 16.9%, JJIT 14.8%; 40% of IT portal hires came via JJIT [vendor] (S33). About 10.0M LinkedIn users in Poland, May 2026 [estimate] (S34). Polish recruiters both source on LinkedIn and post on boards; the own-database channel (28% of hires, S18) means past applicants get called back, so join target companies' talent pools.
- **Tricity:** no Tricity-specific data found. Its software houses are mostly small (2–249 people on GoodFirms listings), so the CTO is usually one message away [assumption]. 2026 meetup calendars not verified.
